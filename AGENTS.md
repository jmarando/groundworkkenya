- Navigation: fixed sidebar above 1100px; grouped dismissible menu below. The menu is src/lib/sections.ts: sections hold several pages as tabs drawn above the page by ConsoleShell (Strategy: next two weeks, road to election day, what the numbers say, elections atlas; Field: voters map, field plan, agents & stipends; Listening & polls), each page keeping its own address; a section shows only the tabs the reader may open (npm test checks no page drops out). Page guards and menus share canOpen() in src/lib/access.ts; agents get field pages only. Why: a short menu, every page still one tap away, and consistent access.
- Campaign isolation: campaign tables have campaign_id default my_campaign(), parent/channel-filling triggers and restrictive own-campaign policies. campaign_members holds one campaign per user; user_roles admin means super admin only. Why: enforce tenancy in the database.
- Campaign hosts show campaign-first home/sign-in; root stays general. Vite allows groundwork.ke and subdomains. Why: branding follows hostname, not stale browser state.
- Root head uses /brand/ icons and field.webmanifest on /field, site.webmanifest elsewhere. public/sw.js caches app/offline pages; IndexedDB saves only queries listed in src/lib/offline/saved.ts, never voter files or feed. The one exception is the walk lists given to the signed-in person: their doors with the names and numbers on file there, deleted when the list ends, at sign-out and after 14 days. Why: safe field use without signal.
- Channels route through unique campaign_channels kind/identifier. Email replies use <slug>+<conversationId>@in.groundwork.ke and /api/public/email/inbound with EMAIL_INBOUND_TOKEN. Why: inbound messages cannot cross campaigns.
- Listening uses Firecrawl gateway web search plus ScrapeCreators TikTok/Reddit/YouTube search; record social failures per sweep without pausing all collection. Why: survive credit outages.
- SMS uses Twilio when a number/service is configured, otherwise Africa’s Talking. /api/public/twilio/* checks TWILIO_CALLBACK_TOKEN because gateway auth hides provider signatures. Why: provider-neutral outbox.
- Home narration uses authenticated speech, streamed PCM and ordered sentence chunks, with deterministic scripts from Home data. Why: consistent natural voice without invented facts.
- Home keeps full morning story beside a stretched today/upcoming diary, secondary news full-width below. Why: at-a-glance planning.
- Individual poll invitations use campaign-scoped contacts, explicit sender permission, opt-outs, per-recipient reservations and managed email without changing consent. Inbox/polls share WhatsApp template fields and server validation; polls permit only poll templates. Why: isolation, approved wording and retry safety.
- Inbox is two tabs (Messages, Channels; ?tab=channels). Messages lists one row per person (src/lib/inbox-people.ts groups their conversations on every channel) with filter chips (All, Needs reply, Unread, Social, Angry), a topic select and search; the thread merges every channel's messages, each with its channel and its status in words, replies go on the channel chosen (the newest by default), WhatsApp past 24 hours offers an approved template instead, opening someone marks them read, and Done/Reopen closes or opens their conversations (markConversations). Call is one press: Twilio rings the campaign's office line (TWILIO_VOICE_FORWARD_TO, the line incoming calls already ring) and connects it to the person, followed live when TWILIO_CALLBACK_TOKEN is set; only with no office line set does it ask for a phone to take the call on. Two-pane on wide screens, list then thread on phones. Why: one person, one conversation, answered from one place.
- Voters uses native MapLibre pan/pinch/wheel and bounded zoom with explained ward/building actions. Zoom levels: area 8–11, constituency 11–13, ward 13–16, street 16–20. Address stores area/house/colour/base/tab; user moves write it, flights do not. Why: predictable controls and reload/Back state.
- Houses use one public/tiles/ PMTiles per campaigns.map_area (none means no map), built by scripts/geo/house-tiles.ts from Google Open Buildings v3, zoom 16, buildings layer, pc, under 25 MiB. Why: complete area read tile by tile from own host.
- Ward (MCA) campaigns: the map area is the ward alone (top "constituency"), its outline and nearest-centre demo map from scripts/geo/ward-map.ts, its houses from house-tiles.ts <cell> <id>. Demos: a campaign's own scenario where its slug has one (src/lib/demo/scenarios: roysambu, githunguri, athwana), else its office's; a demo area with no known position says offMap. The atlas holds the MCA race too (counted in the ward; atlas_elections 20xx-mca), so a ward campaign's Elections view opens on its own race, offered only on wards. Why: a ward race sees its own streets, and no invented positions.
- record_door alone writes house_visits; house_doors()/ward_houses() read colours; reads fail soft before migration. Why: one campaign-scoped retry-safe door record.
- Morning jobs use atomic leases, save dated no-source outcomes, retry only missing/zero-source automated rows hourly and retain provider pauses: a pause keeps the AI off (tried again after 6 hours) but headlines are still saved. Home shows the last week's latest story with news, saying so, until today's is in. Hourly callback catches collection failures and checks mornings before rivals. Why: no blank morning, skipped daily checks or duplicate paid work.
- Archive uses authenticated user-scoped monthly morning/diary reads. Why: historical isolation.
- Public styling stays scoped to .gw-landing with landing-specific workload/phone classes. Why: no console collisions.
- Findings are plain-code rules in src/lib/findings/ over count-only database functions ward_issues, issue_weeks, rival_mention_weeks, ward_spend, ward_supporters, house_doors; return nothing to field agents. Every narrative number is a finding figure. Why: auditable AI grounded only in counts.
- Polling centres: polling_stations holds IEBC's 2022 register (data/stations, schema 27); a position is written only by place_station, use_station_suggestion and station_here, never by a direct update. The map draws the part of the ward nearest each placed centre, worked out in the browser (src/lib/stations/areas.ts), and counts a centre's doors from the houses' plus codes. Why: IEBC publishes no station boundaries, and a position anyone could overwrite would make every number built on it unreliable.
- Walk lists: walk_lists and walk_list_doors (schema 28) are written only by create_walk_list, give_walk_list and close_walk_list; record_door alone writes house_visits, now also at a list's door with no person. A house is on at most one open list, a list is done when every door has a visit since it first went out, and the order of the doors is worked out in the browser when planning. Why: one retry-safe record of each knock, and no two people sent to the same door.
- Path to State House (Strategy): data/kenya holds IEBC's 2022 county rows (Form 34C, the diaspora kept apart) and the 13-region table; src/lib/kenya.ts reads them, src/lib/path.ts applies a region's share or turnout move and checks Article 138 (more than half of the valid votes, 25% in at least 24 of 47 counties), src/lib/path-lines.ts writes the readings with figures. Worked out in the browser: no database, nothing saved. To move a county to another region, change its region in counties.csv (npm test refuses a bad file). Regions are sets of counties, never people: nothing records or guesses anyone's community. Why: Kenyan arithmetic anyone can check, with no voter data and no targeting of communities.
- Path to City Hall (Strategy, governor campaigns): src/lib/seat.ts reads a county's 2022 governor result by constituency from the atlas the page already loads (the two main candidates' votes, the register, the county's totals; seats the figures don't separate are one bucket, the county's total less the others), src/lib/seat-path.ts applies a constituency's two-way share or turnout move and says whether the side has the most votes, src/lib/seat-lines.ts writes the readings with figures. It works for any county the atlas holds by constituency (only Nairobi today; Nyeri has the county total only, so its campaign sees one card saying so); no database, nothing saved. A constituency's "turnout" is the two main candidates' votes over the register because the atlas holds nothing more; every other candidate is held fixed. Places, never people: nothing records or guesses anyone's community. Why: the governor's own arithmetic, auditable from the atlas's rows, without inventing results by ward.
- Path to Parliament (Strategy, MP and MCA campaigns): src/lib/mp-path.ts reads the seat's 2022 result (the two main candidates), its register and each ward's register from the atlas, applies a seat-wide share move or a ward's turnout move (extra voters split as the seat did), and src/lib/mp-path-lines.ts writes the readings with figures; the race before (2017) is read when on file. Results are declared for the seat, so nothing is split by ward. No database, nothing saved. Why: an MP's arithmetic from declared results, without inventing ward results.
- Paid media, step 1 (Broadcast): media_buys (schema 29) holds each ad bought, its ward, dates and "Paid for by" line; booking one posts a pending "ads" expense through the to_ledger trigger and the amount is then fixed. Results (reach, impressions, clicks) are typed in from the platform's report until the platform reads are wired. campaigns.spending_limit_kes is the seat's gazetted limit (Gazette Notice 12251); Finance and Overview say "not on file" rather than borrow another seat's. TikTok takes no paid political ads, so it can't be booked. Why: every shilling of ad spend on the ledger against the right limit.
- Daraja Pulse (Broadcast, "Creators"): Groundwork sends a brief to Pulse's groundwork-brief and reads groundwork-report, server to server with x-job-key (PULSE_FUNCTIONS_URL, PULSE_JOB_KEY), keyed by the Groundwork campaign id; the contract is docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-20-daraja-pulse-link-design.md. The report is read without trust (src/lib/pulse.ts: only https links, malformed rows dropped). record_creator_payouts (schema 30) posts paid payouts once each as pending "creators" expenses. Candidate and managers only. Why: creators briefed and paid with disclosure, every shilling on the ledger.
- Field plan (/field-plan, schema 31): walk_lists.due_on/shift are written only by schedule_walk_list (which can also give the list); field_plan and field_plan_wards read the fortnight for the office; the field app puts today's lists first (src/lib/field-plan.ts). Stipends: stipends_from_field makes one pending stipend per person per day knocked on a walk list, approve_stipends, then record_stipend_paid (receipt by hand) or begin_stipend_payment/finish_stipend_payment (M-Pesa B2C, src/lib/mpesa*.ts, callbacks /api/public/mpesa/b2c-result|b2c-timeout with MPESA_CALLBACK_TOKEN); stipend_paid posts to Finance once. A payout with no answer stays "sending" with a note until someone checks the statement (release_stipend_payment). Agents read only their own stipends. Why: the field team planned by day, and every shilling paid once and on the ledger.
- Diary in calendars (schema 32): my_calendar_feed gives the office a private 64-hex token (made again on reset, which ends the old link); /api/public/calendar/<token>.ics serves calendar_feed_entries (service role only, 30 days back to 4 months ahead, only while the person is still on the office) as iCalendar from src/lib/calendar.ts, Nairobi time, an hour per timed entry. One-way: entries change in the Diary. Why: the diary on everyone's phone without a second place to edit it.
- Listening overview: src/lib/listening-read.ts turns the swept posts into "What to do this week" (angry about you and unhandled, issues rising once last week was swept, a rival warmer than you, rivals' own posts, warm posts to share) and one row per keyword or own account; every card and row opens exactly the posts it counts, with the original link. Why: action, not mood levels, and every number traceable to its posts.
- Access (schema 33): field agents read only the people at the doors of their own open walk lists and people they met in the last 14 days (agent_sees_person), none of conversations/messages, and only their own person_events; record_door and add_person find a number with person_by_phone and make the id before inserting. Office server functions call requireOffice/requireRole (src/lib/require-role.ts) too. campaign_channels identifier/status are set by the super admin only (campaign_channels_guard). Invitations and the super admin role are granted only on a confirmed email. Anything Lovable writes under drizzle/migrations must also go into supabase/migrations (the SQL tests and a fresh database read only that folder). Why: the voter file and inbox stay with the office, and no campaign can take another's line or invitation.
- Inbox channels: the channel card reads what is actually set up (src/lib/channel-status.ts via getChannels: SMS provider and CHANNELS_LIVE, Africa's Talking for USSD, campaign_channels, social_accounts) and holds the social accounts' live switches and "Read moods"; /social redirects to /inbox. Why: no status light says Ready for a channel that isn't.
- Social messages (schema 37): the Meta and X webhooks file each event under the campaign owning the account it came to (social_accounts.external_id for a Page, Instagram account or X user; campaign_channels for a WhatsApp number), falling back to Groundwork's line. Facebook and Instagram replies go out at once through Meta's Graph API (src/lib/meta-send*.ts) with the Page token in social_account_tokens (service role only, set by the super admin with set_social_token from Inbox's channel card); X replies are saved until X's paid API is connected. Why: replies leave from the campaign's own Page, and no campaign can point them at another's.
- Next two weeks (/plan, schema 38): campaign_briefs holds each fortnight's brief (headline, the read in sections marked strength/threat/opening/context, moves by day 1–14 with where/how/measure and an optional diary entry, what to watch), every claim with its source link; a new brief is a new row. src/lib/brief.ts reads it without trust and adds what the campaign's own records flag (open diary days, unplanned walk lists, bare wards, doors knocked) plus the top findings. The first briefs (21 Oct–3 Nov 2026) are in supabase/seeds/briefs-2026-10-21.sql. The read speaks of regions and blocs as polls and the press report them; nothing records or guesses a person's community, and moves never target people by community. Why: one page that tells the candidate what to do this fortnight and why, with the evidence one tap away.
- Road to election day (/roadmap, schema 39): src/lib/election-calendar.ts holds IEBC's 2027 dates (gazetted 20 Aug 2026), each with its source and marked IEBC, the law, or not yet set; nomination and campaign start differ by seat. src/lib/roadmap.ts works out the phases from today, the gaps from the campaign's own counts (agents per stream against 27 Jul 2027, target against the register, consented supporters, doors, appearances, spend against the limit; an unreadable count says nothing) and a starting plan; campaign_roadmaps keeps every AI draft (draftRoadmap), roadmap_items each milestone's status and owner by a stable key, roadmap_notes the office's signed notes. news_impacts holds a national or county story read for the seat (readStory; keywords of kind national/county wait to be read). Nothing is trained: every draft is given the campaign's record (campaignMemory: latest brief, stories read, notes, last draft, 90 days of Listening), and Listening keeps posts three years. Places, never people. Why: a strategy session's plan to polling day, shaped by the campaign's real gaps and the news, with every draft and note on record.
- Voices, spread and reactions (Listening, schema 40): the sweep keeps who posted, views (reach) and shares where TikTok/YouTube/Reddit give them, and refreshes a re-found post's views. src/lib/voices.ts names a post's voice from its author, else the account in its address (Facebook page, X/TikTok handle, subreddit), else the news site; compares platforms (mood, issue, top voices) and finds what is spreading (seen at least the lowest active alert's min_reach, else 5,000, in the last two days). Alerts are evaluated per campaign (never across), may require min_reach, and go by email now (managed sender), in Groundwork ("app"), or WhatsApp once live. rapid_responses keeps each reaction: the AI drafts whether to answer, the angle, the campaign's own reply and a creator brief (rapid.functions.ts, with the campaign's record); the office copies it to creators on WhatsApp or sends it to Daraja Pulse's groundwork-rapid (contract in the Pulse spec; until Pulse builds it, Groundwork says so). A voice is a public account or outlet and only what it published; reactions answer the claim, never the person, and never target anyone by community. Why: the office sees who shapes the seat's conversation on each platform and can answer what spreads within hours.
- Home strategy at a glance (after Today): src/lib/home-strategy.functions.ts reads, softly and for the office only, this week's moves from the brief (movesAhead), the current phase, the next IEBC date (nextKeyDate) and the two most pressing gaps, posts spreading past the lowest alert bar in the last two days with drafted reactions waiting, and the latest wider story read for the seat; StrategyBlock shows four short cards, each opening its page. Why: the morning shows what the strategy says now without making Home long.
- X search (schema 41): X keywords are searched through ScrapeCreators' /v1/twitter/search/posts (the key Listening already uses; search-engine discovery, so posts older than a week are dropped) or, when TWITTERAPI_IO_KEY is set, TwitterAPI.io's advanced search (X's own Latest) first (src/lib/x-search.server.ts), both read without trust by src/lib/x-read.ts (an answer in a shape not read is reported in the sweep's notes); the hourly X pass (src/lib/x-sweep.server.ts) takes the X_TOPICS_PER_HOUR (default 12) keywords searched longest ago (listening_topics.x_scanned_at), one call each from the shared "x" budget (X_DAILY_SEARCHES, default 300), and keeps posts with author, views and reposts under the keyword's campaign. The hourly job also reads each campaign's newest unread county or national story for its seat (autoReadWiderNews, two a day at most; the memory it gives the AI is scoped to that campaign because the job runs as the service role). Listen reads the fortnight's headline, the next IEBC date and the latest wider story. Why: X is where Kenya's opinions are shaped, and wider news reaches every seat's morning without anyone pressing a button.
- Broadcast and calls (schema 42): Broadcast is tabs (Message people, Auto-calls, Paid ads, Creators, Sent, ?tab=) over one "Who" filter; each channel card counts the people in it who agreed to that channel. Every Twilio call is a voice_calls row followed by Twilio's reports at /api/public/twilio/call-status (?call=id, ?leg=voter for the Inbox call's second leg) and described by src/lib/call-status.ts (Twilio error codes in plain words; no report for three minutes says so). Auto-calls (voice_blasts; src/lib/autocall*.ts) play the candidate's cloned voice (campaign_voices, ElevenLabs, ELEVENLABS_API_KEY, kept with the candidate's consent note), an uploaded recording or a standard voice (English only), stored in the public voice-messages bucket; /api/public/twilio/autocall plays it inside a Gather, opens with "a recorded message from the campaign" unless turned off, and 9 withdraws the person's consent_call. Only people who agreed to calls and haven't opted out are rung, each once per auto-call (voice_calls_once_idx), test call to yourself first. Sample contacts (tags 'sample') are never messaged or called by any bulk sender: queue_broadcast and broadcast_estimate skip them (schema 43), and the WhatsApp and auto-call senders filter them out. WhatsApp sends approved templates with a test to your own number and pictures uploaded to broadcast-media. Why: calls that say where they are, a candidate's voice used only with consent, and promotional messages that are easy to check before they go.
- One thread per person (schema 44): every campaign writes from the same WhatsApp number, so a WhatsApp reply is filed under the campaign that last wrote to that number on WhatsApp within 30 days (waReplyCampaign in src/lib/whatsapp.server.ts), else the number's owner, as SMS replies already are (inbound.server.ts); conversation threads are unique per campaign (conversations_thread_uniq on campaign_id, platform, external_thread_id). Every outgoing WhatsApp or SMS with a person is put in that person's conversation in the sending campaign as it is recorded (message_into_conversation trigger, conversation_for), so broadcasts and invitations show in the Inbox and replies land under them; sample contacts are left alone. Why: whatever a campaign sends and whatever comes back reads as one conversation in that campaign's Inbox.
- Connecting a Page: Inbox's channel card (super admin) takes a Page access token (connectPage in src/lib/channels.functions.ts): Meta's /me says which Page and linked Instagram account it is, the open campaign's facebook/instagram social_accounts are kept with those ids (external_id, so the Meta webhook files their messages here) and the token (set_social_token), and the Page is subscribed to the app's messages. The card says when META_APP_SECRET/META_VERIFY_TOKEN aren't set and gives the webhook address. Why: a campaign's Page connected in one step, its DMs landing in its own Inbox.
